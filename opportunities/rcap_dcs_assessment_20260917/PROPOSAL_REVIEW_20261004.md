## Executive summary

Token Junkie Labs proposes a four-week, vendor-neutral assessment of RCAP's DCS. The assessment will produce a practical recommendation for stabilizing, modernizing, replacing, integrating or deliberately retaining parts of the system. The work begins with RCAP's operating needs and available evidence; it does not presume that replacement or a particular vendor is the answer.

The engagement centers on the DCS. Adjacent systems and data flows enter the assessment where they affect a DCS decision. Recommendations will distinguish the evidence that supports an option, the assumptions it depends on, the remaining uncertainties and the next decision RCAP would need to make.

## Discovery and assessment approach

Kickoff will confirm RCAP's decision questions, success criteria, available materials, access constraints, session participants and a shared calendar. RCAP will be asked to identify the owner for each requested item rather than gather everything before useful work starts. An evidence register will record what was received, its source, the decision it informs and any unresolved gap.

The six stakeholder sessions are organized around these groups:

1. Programs - workflows, service delivery, field constraints and operational priorities.
2. Reporting - reporting obligations, data ownership, SQL dependence and reconciliation.
3. Training, events and communications - scheduling, outreach and related DCS handoffs.
4. Finance - DCS-to-Unanet GovCon requirements, reconciliation and operational ownership.
5. DCS Workgroup and regional representatives - shared-system needs, workflow variation, support and adoption.
6. Leadership - strategic priorities, tradeoffs, decision criteria and sequencing.

Participants can be combined within a session where RCAP prefers. Technical and security owners can join relevant discussions or supply focused follow-up information. The planned six sessions do not promise a separate interview with every region or individual user.

The current-state work uses available documentation, demonstrations, stakeholder discussions and selected technical material to explain the system's strengths and constraints. An observed condition will be labeled as documented evidence, stakeholder report, assumption or unresolved question. Missing access or documentation will be recorded with its effect on a recommendation rather than presented as an established defect.

Each option will be evaluated against agreed decision criteria, including operational fit, supportability, delivery dependencies, resiliency, data and reporting implications, effort and risk. Cost ranges or estimates will carry their assumptions. The final roadmap will name the next decisions, their dependencies and practical actions RCAP can take during the following 90 days.
